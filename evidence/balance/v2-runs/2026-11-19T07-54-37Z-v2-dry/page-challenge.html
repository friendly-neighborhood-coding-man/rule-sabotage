Origin: operator (Eric (V2 specification), formalised by Claude). Proposed mechanism: Placing movement boosts on one's own route is a permissible, strong strategy. The V1 'dominant effect' and 'penalty that rarely hurts' metrics flag it because they measure frequency and the installer's prior, not whether the play is a defect.

Even (0.5) vs Intervention: Planner valuation only: benefit from own-installed traps ignored [head-to-head]. Measurement: Head-to-head: win rate of the treated planner vs the control planner (marginal treatment impact). Predicted to be lower with the change.

Section H1: Intrinsic rule geometry favours forward progress

Origin: operator (Eric (V2 specification), formalised by Claude). Proposed mechanism: Teleport sends a piece to a random empty MIDDLE square (columns B-F). Pieces activate traps near their own start, about 6 squares from goal, so a middle destination is on average forward for any activator, whoever installed it.

Section H2: Planner exploitation through its own valuation

Origin: operator (Eric (V2 specification), formalised by Claude). Proposed mechanism: The planner's engine-simulated score lowers its own pieces' path cost when they can ride its own traps, so it builds conveyor belts (Shove east 2 for Human, Shove west 2 for AI) and teleports on its own route. The behaviour comes from the simulated reward, not from the V0.3 prior.
