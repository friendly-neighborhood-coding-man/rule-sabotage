Uncertainty and limitations

  • With 20 decisive games a first-mover share must reach at least this many wins (75%) before an exact test rejects a 50/50 split at 5%, and a 95% interval is roughly +/- 20 points wide. Smaller real advantages are invisible at this sample size.
  • Five conditions were screened at once; at least one "significant-looking" difference is expected by chance alone now and then.
  • The same seeds are used in every condition, but the games diverge quickly (different piece counts change every plan), so pairs share a starting RNG, not a game.
  • Both sides are the same deterministic planner with the same weights. "Human side" means the side moving left to right, not a human player. Results describe this planner against itself.
  • Changing the piece count changes three things at once: pieces on the board, actions per turn and the victory requirement. These games cannot separate them.
  • Within one condition the 20 games are 10 seeds × 2 first movers; the two games of a seed are not fully independent.
  • These 100 games are exploratory. Any hypothesis formed from them must be tested on fresh seeds, and any correction judged on a separate sealed holdout.
  • The planner's look-ahead uses a copy of the engine RNG (it can foresee teleports). V2 tested whether that matters for balance and found no support, but it remains part of how these games were played.
